# Understanding XIRR and Early Stage Volatility

**XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return)** is a mathematical metric that calculates the annualized rate of return for multiple cash flows occurring on different irregular dates.

1. **Why XIRR is Used for SIPs:** Unlike a one-time lump-sum investment (where simple CAGR works), a SIP involves regular contributions month after month. Each instalment is invested for a different duration. XIRR normalizes these separate cash flows into an annualized percentage.
2. **Why Early Numbers Swing Wildly:**
   - In mathematics, annualizing return over a very short window (1 to 6 months) acts like a magnifying glass. 
   - A tiny 2% market fluctuation over 15 days can mathematically extrapolate into a +45% or -35% annualized XIRR figure.
   - For short histories (under 12 months or fewer than 3 instalments), XIRR is mathematically volatile and does not represent long-term performance.
3. **Contribution Progress vs Returns:** In the first year of a SIP, focusing on contribution consistency (instalments made and units accumulated) is more grounded than tracking short-term annualized percentage swings.
